iCall Helpline Guidelines and FAQ (TISS)

Introduction:
iCall is a psycho-social helpline started by the Tata Institute of Social Sciences (TISS) to provide counseling services to individuals in distress. The services are confidential, non-judgmental, and run by trained professional counselors.

Common Situations & Response Protocols:

1. Handling Relationship Distress:
   - Counselor Role: Act as a facilitator, not a decision-maker. Avoid giving direct advice like "you should break up" or "you must stay together".
   - Key Technique: Active Listening and Validation. Help the caller identify their emotions (fear, sadness, anger) and list their options.
   - Sample Script: "It sounds like you are feeling very overwhelmed by this conflict. Let's look at the options you have and explore how each one might make you feel."

2. Dealing with Academic and Career Stress:
   - Common issues: Fear of failure, parental pressure, imposter syndrome.
   - Response Protocol: Help callers separate their self-worth from their academic/career achievements. Address immediate physiological anxiety first (using breathing exercises) before discussing career planning.
   - Sample Script: "I hear how much pressure you're under. It's completely understandable to feel anxious. Before we look at solutions, let's take a few deep breaths together to help calm our bodies."

3. Addressing Anxiety and Panic Attacks:
   - Protocol: Focus on grounding techniques to bring the caller back to the present moment. Use the 5-4-3-2-1 sensory method.
   - 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding Method:
     - Name 5 things you can see around you.
     - Name 4 things you can physically touch.
     - Name 3 things you can hear.
     - Name 2 things you can smell.
     - Name 1 thing you can taste.
   - Sample Script: "You are safe right now. Let's focus on this room. Tell me 5 things you can see around you."

Confidentiality Policy:
- Everything discussed remains private.
- The ONLY exception is when there is an imminent risk of harm to the caller's life or someone else's life. In such cases, counselors will break confidentiality to contact emergency support or family, as per standard ethical guidelines.
