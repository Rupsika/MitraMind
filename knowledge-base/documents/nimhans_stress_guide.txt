NIMHANS Stress Management and Coping Guide

What is Stress?
Stress is a natural physical and mental reaction to life experiences. Everyone experiences stress from time to time. Anything from everyday responsibilities like work and family to serious life events such as a new diagnosis, war, or the death of a loved one can trigger stress. For immediate, short-term situations, stress can be beneficial to your health. It can help you cope with potentially serious situations. Your body responds to stress by releasing hormones that increase your heart and breathing rates and ready your muscles to respond.

Healthy Coping Strategies:
1. Deep Breathing:
   - Sit comfortably with your back straight.
   - Inhale slowly through your nose for 4 seconds.
   - Hold your breath for 4 seconds.
   - Exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 seconds.
   - Repeat this cycle 5-10 times. This stimulates the vagus nerve and triggers the body's relaxation response.
2. Cognitive Reframing:
   - Identify negative or catastrophizing thoughts (e.g., "Everything is ruined").
   - Challenge these thoughts: Is there evidence for this? Is there a more balanced way to view the situation?
   - Replace with realistic thoughts (e.g., "This is a setback, but I can handle it step by step").
3. Structured Problem Solving:
   - Define the problem clearly.
   - Brainstorm all possible solutions without judging them.
   - Evaluate the pros and cons of each solution.
   - Select the best option and create a concrete step-by-step action plan.
4. Time Management:
   - Break large, overwhelming tasks into smaller, manageable chunks.
   - Prioritize tasks using the Eisenhower Matrix (Urgent vs. Important).
   - Learn to say "No" to demands that exceed your capacity.

When to Seek Professional Help:
You should consult a mental health professional (psychologist or psychiatrist) if:
- Feeling overwhelmed, anxious, or sad persists for more than two weeks.
- Stress interferes significantly with your ability to perform daily tasks at work, school, or home.
- You experience physical symptoms like chronic headaches, digestive issues, or racing heartbeat without an underlying medical cause.
- You turn to alcohol, drugs, or other unhealthy behaviors to cope.
- You experience thoughts of self-harm or ending your life.
